Keep your hands on the edit

Select a shot. Make your move.

Cut several camera angles together, choose which shot sits in front, and save without breaking your flow.

Switch cameras as the music plays.

Record your angles, stop and leave the video lanes disarmed. Start playback, click inside CamOrder, then press 1–9 for the numbered lanes. Each switch cuts every region crossing that moment and brings your chosen camera forward. Choose Live cuts: Free for the playhead position, or Snap for the nearest beat or subdivision. Undo reverses the whole switch.

Drag the grip beside a lane name to change its position and live-switch number. While paused, number keys assign layers to selected regions instead.

Click inside CamOrder’s editor to use these editing shortcuts. While typing a name or value, keys stay in that field; press Return or click outside a lane name to finish editing it. ⌘ means Command; ⇧ means Shift; ⌥ means Option.

Selection is for editing; layer numbers set the picture. The numbered badge and colored border show a region’s output layer. A yellow outline shows what you’ve selected. Resize or move a foreground shot to reveal other videos behind it. You can also assign layers with the Output Layer control.

Get started

Add CamOrder to your Logic session.

CamOrder runs as an Audio Unit effect. Use one instance on Stereo Out for your whole video project.

  1. Download the DMG and open it. Double-click Install CamOrder Studio.pkg and follow the installer; it chooses the plug-in folder for you. Fully quit and reopen Logic after installing.
  2. On Stereo Out, choose Audio FX → Audio Units → Santismo → CamOrder Studio → Stereo.
  3. Open the project menu beside CamOrder, choose New Project…, and save the CamOrder project alongside your Logic project. Keep the whole .camorderstudio folder when moving or sharing it; it contains the video media.

Then follow the recording steps below to choose cameras and start a take.

No MIDI or timecode-audio routing is needed for normal Stereo Out operation. Audio passes through unchanged; recorded takes are video-only. Import a bounced master if you want audio in the exported movie.

Two kinds of arming, one session

Record your music and your cameras.

Logic’s armed tracks capture the audio. CamOrder’s armed lanes capture the video. Set them up once for each take, then work from Logic’s timeline.

  1. Choose your camera inputs. In CamOrder’s Live Inputs, select a Default camera or screen source and allow access when macOS asks. Every lane starts with that input. For multiple angles, choose a different camera in each lane’s input menu. Wait for the live previews before starting.
  2. Arm the video lanes you want to capture. Click Arm on each CamOrder lane for this take. Arming a Logic audio track does not arm a CamOrder lane.
  3. Choose what Logic should do. To record a new performance, also record-enable the relevant audio tracks in Logic, position Logic’s playhead, and press Record. To film along with music already in the project, leave those audio tracks unarmed and press Play. Armed CamOrder lanes record video in either case.
  4. Stay in the music. With CamOrder on Stereo Out, you can work in Logic’s timeline or close the plug-in editor during a take. When the editor is active, R forwards Logic’s R key command and Space controls Play/Stop. CamOrder’s red Record and Play buttons also control Logic. If you’ve customized Logic’s key commands, use its transport controls.
  5. Stop, review and rearm. Stop Logic to finish the video take. CamOrder automatically disarms its lanes; arm them again for the next take. Leave them disarmed when you only want to watch and edit existing footage. If a take remains active after Logic stops, use Stop Take in CamOrder.
  6. Save and export. Save CamOrder with ⌘ S or Save, and save your Logic project too. Edit the regions, export the movie, and import it into Logic’s movie lane at the start specified in the export’s placement note.

For the next camera take, start with Logic stopped and its playhead at the intended position. Automatic repeated cycle takes are not supported. Use the camera sync guide below to measure and correct a consistent camera delay.

Fine-tune your camera sync

Measure the delay. Dial it in.

Different cameras can arrive at different times. Film Logic’s clock for a quick test, then let CamOrder’s Sync Calculator work out the correction in milliseconds.

  1. Show milliseconds in Logic. Set Logic’s time display to hours, minutes, seconds and milliseconds, for example 00:00:36.240. Use that display for both readings, rather than bars, frames or samples.
  2. Film the clock with the camera you want to calibrate. Point it at Logic’s time display. Select that camera in CamOrder, arm its lane, and record a short test while Logic runs. For a first calibration, start with the project and lane offsets at 0 ms.
  3. Export, import and compare. Export the test and load it into Logic’s movie lane. Position it at the exact start in CamOrder’s placement note. Pause on a clear frame and read the actual Logic clock and the clock visible inside the recorded picture.
  4. Let the calculator do the math. Open CamOrder → Sync → Calculator, choose Milliseconds, and enter both readings. Choose the camera’s lane for an individual correction, or Whole project for a shared adjustment. Click Calculate, review the result, then Apply offset.
  5. Re-export and check at the same position. The correction works on footage already recorded and appears in Main Stage playback and new exports. Replace the test movie in Logic, keep the same movie start, and compare again. Existing exported files do not change automatically.

For example: the camera is 80 ms late

Logic reads 00:00:36.240, while the clock filmed inside the video reads 00:00:36.160. The correction is −80 ms, moving the video earlier. A positive correction moves it later.

The calculator adds the correction to the current offset. If you already have −20 ms applied, another −80 ms correction proposes −100 ms. Review that value before applying.

Lane and project offsets add together. Calibrate each camera’s lane separately when their delays differ. Check a few points in the test and recheck after changing cameras or capture settings: one offset corrects a consistent delay, not drift. Original recordings remain unchanged.
